Technical context
Lite creates successor lifecycle archives by default and exports successor .qcont shards, a signable .archive-state.json, and a mutable .lifecycle-bundle.json. Without an external archive-approval signature over the archive-state descriptor, restore verifies integrity only and does not claim archive approval. Same-state resharing stays in Pro as an optional maintenance tool and does not replace archive approval while the archive state stays unchanged.

Custody and archive exports

Recovery normally depends on independent shard custody and the exported JSON descriptors, not on keeping ML-KEM container keys in the browser or on disk.

  • Store QVqcont-7 shards across separate locations or custodians so one loss does not destroy recovery.
  • Retain the archive-state descriptor and lifecycle bundle JSON; restore, review, and evidence workflows depend on them.
  • Archive approval happens outside the browser: sign the canonical archive-state descriptor bytes, then use Pro Attach to bind detached signatures and related evidence to the lifecycle bundle.
  • Backup Keys is optional convenience. Some operators or auditors must not retain ML-KEM keys—skip it when your custody policy forbids keeping container keys.

    Configuration Tips
    More availability = reduce k or t;
    More confidentiality = increase m.
    If someone controls t shards they can decrypt; with fewer than t shards they cannot.
    m — in the context of RS is the number of redundant parity shards.
    t — in the context of SSS, this is the threshold: the minimum number of Shamir shards required to recover the secret.
